About Kapantay

Kapantay is an independent educational project on gender and society. In Filipino, kapantay means equal, level, on par. That is the goal.

Why gender-based violence

Every gender project needs a clear focus. Kapantay centres on violence against women because it is a major public health and gender equality problem and a violation of women’s human rights.[1] It is also measurable, covered by strong Philippine laws, and preventable, which makes it a good place to turn awareness into action.

Around it, the site covers the wider themes of equality, inclusion, empowerment and social responsibility, because violence cannot be understood apart from them.

The project in one paragraph

A dashboard, an issue guide, an evidence library, a help page and an action plan, all built around one question: what do we know about violence against women, and what can each of us do about it? Everything links to a source, and everything runs in your browser.

How we source and write

Every number is cited
Each statistic carries a numbered reference that opens the full source on the Evidence page, with a button to copy the citation.
Official sources first
We prefer WHO, the Philippine Statistics Authority, the Philippine Commission on Women, UN Women and the World Economic Forum. Where a figure comes through a news report or law-firm summary, the source list says so.
Say what a number measures
Prevalence figures depend on who was asked and how. We state the group, the year and the definition, and we warn against comparing figures from different surveys.
Respect for survivors
No graphic detail and no sensational framing. Every page keeps a route to help within reach.
Dated and reviewable
Content was last reviewed on 19 September 2026. Statistics change, so always check the linked source for the latest figures. Found an error? Check the source first, then report it to the site maintainer.

Logo and design

The mark

An equals sign inside a ring of three segments. The equals sign is the goal, equality. One of the three segments is highlighted, echoing the “1 in 3” women worldwide who experience violence.[1] Orange is the colour used in the UN’s global campaign against violence against women.

Privacy and your data

Kapantay has no server, no analytics and no cookies. Everything you do is saved in your own browser’s storage on this device:

  • Your theme choice (light or dark)
  • If you create an account: your name, email and a salted hash of your password
  • Your progress, quiz score and action plan

None of it is sent anywhere. Because this is a demonstration, the sign-in is not protected like a real service. Do not reuse a real password, and use a private window on a shared device.

Clear everything

This deletes every demo account, progress record and action plan saved in this browser, and signs you out.
